Engineering notes

Setbacks and open space
6′ front lawn plus a 12′ × 20′ car porch. The 13′ × 11′ rear courtyard is open to the sky, so the kitchen, bedrooms and lounges get daylight even with party walls on both sides.
Levels
Plinth 1′-6″ above road level to keep out rain water. 11′ floor to floor, 10′-6″ clear ceiling, 6″ RCC slabs.
Structure
RCC frame with 12″ × 12″ columns at about 15′ spacing. Walls are 9″ brick outside and 4½″ inside. Upper-floor partitions that don't sit on a wall below are carried on beams.
Plumbing stacked
Every first-floor bath sits directly above a ground-floor bath, so each pair shares one vertical shaft. That means fewer pipes, fewer leaks and easier repairs.
Staircase
Dog-leg stair, 4′ wide flights, 22 risers of 6″ with 10″ treads, continuing to a mumty on the roof.
Light and air
Every bedroom has an outside window or balcony door. The first-floor bath behind Bedroom 2 is internal and needs an exhaust duct to the roof.
Roof
Room for about 10 × 550 W solar panels (≈5.5 kW) and a 1,000 L water tank on the mumty. Use brick-tile heat-proofing with foam insulation over the slab.

The plot size assumes 1 marla = 225 sq ft (LDA, DHA and most societies). If your society uses 272 sq ft, 7 marla is about 1,906 sq ft and the plan stretches in depth. Check setbacks and covered-area limits with your authority, and have a licensed structural engineer size the footings, columns and steel from a soil test before you build.
